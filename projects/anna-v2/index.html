Why ANNA Exists

Give every conversation a dependable operational home.

Customer-support work often begins in a shared inbox but quickly spreads across labels, spreadsheets, manual follow-ups, and individual memory. Replies can be mistaken for new requests, ownership becomes unclear, and reporting loses the thread connecting the customer’s message to the work performed by the team.

ANNA was designed around the familiar idea of a dependable receptionist named Anna: someone who receives each request, remembers its context, and directs it to the right place. The system brings that discipline to email operations without pretending that automation replaces the support agent.

Gmail remains the conversation authority. n8n controls scheduled intake and orchestration, while Google Apps Script applies the operational rules around a Sheets-based ticketing layer. Agents work from prioritized views and return to the authoritative Gmail thread through each Ticket ID.

Project, implementation, release The project is ANNA. Its current implementation is ANNA v2, and the production-verified release documented here is v2.3.2. It is deterministic and AI-ready—not AI-powered. The planned AI layer begins with ANNA v3.

System Design

Conversation identity first. Operational action second.

The workflow resolves whether a message belongs to an existing conversation before any ticket action occurs. That decision protects continuity across replies, monthly databases, lifecycle changes, and agent workspaces.

01 · Schedule

Controlled intake

Business-hours and after-hours schedules fetch unread Gmail messages in bounded batches.

02 · Normalize

Chronological batch

n8n orders and normalizes the messages before sending them through the ticketing logic.

03 · Resolve

Conversation lookup

Gmail Thread ID and the global Conversation_Index determine whether the request is new or existing.

04 · New

Route and register

A new conversation is labeled, routed, assigned a Ticket ID, and linked across the operating records.

05 · Existing

Update authority

A reply resolves to its original ticket—even across months—and advances the correct lifecycle record.

06 · Operate

Queue and maintain

Agent views, SLA calculations, audit logs, dashboards, closure rules, and maintenance stay synchronized.

Reliability shaped the architecture Early v1.x testing exposed the risk of concurrent writes to a Sheets-based database. ANNA v2 moved to a controlled batching and queue model. The current configuration uses a 15-message fetch limit, a 10-minute business-hours schedule, an hourly after-hours schedule, and one active v2.3.2 scheduler. Daily maintenance runs at 5:00 a.m.; automatic closure uses seven calendar days, while 12 hours is the after-hours acknowledgement cooldown.

Operational Layer

One conversation, several purpose-built views.

Each structure has a distinct job. The system avoids treating a single sheet as inbox, database, queue, customer record, and report at the same time.

Monthly Tickets

The authoritative ticket records for intake, ownership, status, timestamps, SLA, and resolution.

Conversation_Index

A global map from Gmail thread to authoritative ticket, preserving continuity across monthly databases.

Customer Master

A synchronized customer-level view that aggregates contact information and ticket history.

TODAY’S QUEUE

An editable workspace for NEW, OPEN, and CUSTOMER REPLIED work, with controlled assignment and status fields.

FOLLOW_UP

A focused view for PENDING tickets that need a later customer or agent action.

Dashboard + Audit

Operational reporting, aging, traceable changes, guardrails, and maintenance records for review and handoff.

Ticket Identity & Lifecycle

Readable for people. Stable for the system.

Ticket IDs follow a configurable department, date, and daily-sequence pattern—for example, SUP-08092026-0001. In the agent views, the ID is also a direct link to the Gmail conversation, keeping the working inbox and the operational record connected.

Guarded status transitions preserve a consistent lifecycle: NEW → OPEN → PENDING → CUSTOMER REPLIED → OPEN → SOLVED → CLOSED. The same model handles reopening, pending duration, business-calendar SLA time, after-hours acknowledgement, cooldown rules, and automatic closure.

This separation matters: the monthly ticket record controls operational state, the Conversation_Index controls cross-period resolution, and Gmail controls the actual conversation. No one layer has to impersonate all three.

Production Verification

Correctness held across new, existing, and cross-month work.

The v2.3.2 validation processed 15 messages in one execution: 12 new conversations and three replies to existing tickets. The checks below focus on observable system behavior rather than feature claims.

Behavior Observed evidence Result
New-conversation intake 12 messages were routed, labeled, assigned unique Ticket IDs, and registered across the required records. Passed
Existing-conversation updates Three replies updated their authoritative tickets instead of creating new ones. Passed
Duplicate prevention The completed 15-message batch produced zero duplicate tickets. Passed
Cross-month continuity A reply resolved through Conversation_Index to its ticket in the correct monthly database. Passed
After-hours handling Scheduler separation, acknowledgement rules, and cooldown behavior were validated without changing ticket authority. Passed
Customer and agent views Customer Master, queues, lifecycle fields, links, and reporting stayed aligned with the ticket records. Passed
Host interruption recovery An abrupt host shutdown and restart preserved the persistent environment and allowed operations to resume. Passed
Measured baseline, not a throughput promise The full execution completed in 9m 19.919s—about 37 seconds per message. That measurement establishes observed v2.3.2 behavior in the tested environment. Performance optimization was deliberately deferred until after correctness and operational safety were proven.
