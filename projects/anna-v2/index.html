Project Overview

Deterministic first. AI-ready by design.

ANNA handles the predictable administrative work around customer-support email: monitoring messages, preserving conversation state, routing requests, registering tickets, synchronizing records, supporting agent action, and maintaining lifecycle and reporting data.

Without that operational layer, teams repeatedly decide whether a message is new, search for earlier context, update multiple records, and manually keep email and ticket state aligned. ANNA converts those repeated decisions into explicit, auditable rules.

ANNA v2.3.2 is AI-ready, not AI-powered. Ticket identity, conversation authority, routing, lifecycle, SLA calculations, and database state are deterministic. Natural-language intelligence is deliberately reserved for ANNA v3.

Portfolio-safe demonstration The system uses test customer data. Departments, labels, schedules, SLAs, ticket formats, and workflow rules are configurable for different business requirements.

Current Architecture

From unread email to authoritative agent work.

n8n owns scheduled intake and orchestration. Google Apps Script enforces the operational rules around the Sheets-based ticketing and agent layer.

01 · Schedule

Controlled intake

Business-hours processing runs every 10 minutes; after-hours processing runs hourly.

02 · Gmail

Unread batch

Up to 15 unread messages are normalized, validated, and sorted chronologically.

03 · Resolve

Conversation authority

Thread IDs and Conversation_Index determine whether the message is new or existing.

04 · New

Route and register

New conversations receive a department, Gmail label, Ticket ID, and monthly record.

05 · Existing

Update in place

Replies resolve across monthly databases and update the authoritative ticket.

06 · Operate

Agent and maintenance layer

Queues, lifecycle, SLA, reporting, audit, and daily maintenance stay synchronized.

Two time rules, two different purposes Automatic closure eligibility is 7 calendar days. The 12-hour rule is the after-hours reply cooldown—not an auto-close threshold. Daily maintenance runs at 5:00 AM.

Production Capabilities

More than Gmail connected to a spreadsheet.

ANNA maintains state across conversations, monthly databases, agent edits, and maintenance cycles while keeping the original Gmail thread authoritative and accessible.

01 · CONVERSATION

Conversation-aware ticketing

Gmail Thread IDs and a global Conversation_Index prevent replies from becoming duplicate tickets.

02 · ROUTING

Rules-based intake

Messages are processed chronologically and routed to configurable departments with matching Gmail labels.

03 · AGENT WORK

Editable operational queues

TODAY’S QUEUE and FOLLOW_UP expose assignments, statuses, Last Agent Reply, and linked source conversations.

04 · LIFECYCLE

SLA and after-hours handling

Guarded transitions, reopen logic, pending duration, acknowledgements, cooldowns, and auto-close follow explicit rules.

05 · REPORTING

Customer and management data

Customer Master consolidates history while the Dashboard reports status, department, priority, and aging.

06 · RELIABILITY

Guardrails and traceability

Document locking, schema checks, audit records, cross-sheet lookup, and persistent storage protect operations.

Operational Data Layer

One authoritative record, several useful views.

Google Sheets is the current persistence and agent-operations layer. Ticket identity and lifecycle rules are kept conceptually separate from the storage choice so the datastore can evolve later.

Monthly Tickets

Authoritative ticket records with identity, customer, lifecycle, SLA, activity, and closure data.

Conversation_Index

Global thread-to-ticket resolution across monthly ticket databases.

Customer Master

Consolidated ticket counts and customer history without inflating totals on replies.

TODAY’S QUEUE

Materialized workspace for NEW, OPEN, and CUSTOMER REPLIED work.

FOLLOW_UP

Focused operational workspace for PENDING tickets that need later action.

Dashboard + Audit

Management reporting plus traceable setup, assignment, status, guardrail, and recovery activity.

Validated 15-Message Test

Tested as a system, not one message at a time.

The production-verification run exercised the configured Gmail fetch limit and mixed new and existing conversations in the same execution.

Test condition Observed behavior Result
12 brand-new conversations 12 unique tickets were routed, linked, registered, and added to TODAY’S QUEUE. Passed
3 replies to existing conversations All three updated their authoritative tickets instead of creating duplicates. Passed
Cross-month reply Conversation_Index resolved a reply to its ticket stored in another monthly database. Passed
After-hours execution New tickets and existing replies received their correct eligible acknowledgements. Passed
Customer Master reconciliation Ticket totals increased by exactly 12; the three replies did not inflate counts. Passed
Abrupt host shutdown recovery Docker, n8n, and the persistent n8n_data volume recovered without stored workflow loss. Passed
Measured baseline, not a throughput promise The 9m 19.919s execution is an observed baseline. Capacity depends on traffic distribution, connected-service latency, and future optimization; it does not prove that the system is limited to 15 messages per 10 minutes.

Feature Spotlight

A Ticket ID designed for actual agent use.

ANNA does not generate an arbitrary number. Its structured identifier can communicate the department, creation date, and daily sequence while linking directly back to the source Gmail conversation.

SUP-08092026-0001 SUP = Support · 08092026 = creation date · 0001 = first Support ticket for that date.

Demonstration codes include SUP for Support, BIL for Billing, SAL for Sales, and MAN for Manual Review. The prefix, date format, sequence length, and numbering scope are all configurable per client requirement.

Ticket IDs in the operational sheets are clickable hyperlinks, allowing an agent to move from the structured database directly to the authoritative Gmail thread.
